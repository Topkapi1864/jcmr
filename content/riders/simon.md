I'm the middle one of three children and, as my brother and sister say, I was always ‘Mum’s favourite’!

I'm married to Leanne, who I suspect has heard enough about cycling to last her several lifetimes. I couldn't have taken on this challenge without her unwavering support, patience and belief in me.

I'm 57 years old and have always enjoyed sport and keeping active. At 31, I ran the London Marathon, and at 35, I cycled a 150-mile stage of the Tour de France route with my brother. Endurance has never come naturally to me, and that ride through France remains the most physically demanding challenge I've ever undertaken. I suffered considerably, both during the ride and for quite some time afterwards, and I distinctly remember Gary and I telling ourselves, ‘Never again!’

My first hip replacement came when I was just 40, followed by a replacement on my other side a few years later. Unfortunately, that left hip subsequently came loose and had to be replaced again eight years ago. It has since deteriorated for reasons that remain unclear, and I'm now awaiting my fourth hip replacement.

I won't pretend the training has been enjoyable. There have been some incredibly tough days, plenty of low moments and more occasions than I'd care to admit when I've questioned whether I can actually do this.

But whenever those doubts creep in, I remind myself why I'm doing it.

I'm doing this for Mum.

She was the strongest, kindest and most beautiful woman I have ever known.

I suspect she wouldn't be particularly happy with me putting myself through all of this! But I also know that, despite her worries, she'd be behind me every mile of the way.

More than anything, I just hope I can make her proud.
