On 23rd February 2024, my brother Gary, my sister Sarah and I took our Mum, Janet Channon, to St James’s University Hospital to get the results of a scan she’d had the week before.

Nothing could have prepared us for the news we received that day.

Mum had stage 4 stomach cancer and we were told there was nothing that could be done. It was absolutely devastating.

I won’t go into all the details of what followed, but we took Mum to London, where she started chemotherapy and immunotherapy. Incredibly, by July she was well enough to drive herself all the way back home to Addingham to celebrate her birthday surrounded by her family.

And that was Mum.

Despite everything she was going through, she kept going. She was able to see her granddaughter Yasmin get married in Wiltshire, and she even travelled to France for the wedding of her niece Melissa.

Those are memories that our family will treasure forever.

Sadly, the cancer returned, and this time it was much more aggressive.

On 15th November 2024, Mum passed away at my brother Gary’s home in Castelnau, Barnes, London, with her three children by her side.

## Mum, Nan and Sister

Mum was a truly inspirational woman. She was beautiful in every sense of the word.

She came from a large family of 13, and being a sister was a huge part of her life. The majority of the family spent their lives in and around Yeadon, and Mum remained incredibly close to her brothers and sisters throughout her life. She saw them virtually every week.

Those relationships mattered enormously to her. She wasn't simply one of 13 children who had grown up and gone their separate ways. They remained a close family, sharing their lives with one another, and Mum loved being a sister just as much as she loved being surrounded by her wider family.

Family really was everything to her.

Gary, Sarah and I couldn't have asked for a better Mum. She was the most caring, loving, generous and kind woman you could ever wish to meet, and she absolutely doted on the three of us.

We were her life, and she was such a huge part of ours.

But to another generation of our family, she was Nan.

Being Nan to our children was a role Mum absolutely loved. She adored her grandchildren and they adored her. Seeing them grow up and being part of their lives meant the world to her.

She was a Mum, a Nan, a sister and so much more to so many people. Her absence is felt throughout a very large and very close family, and she is missed every single day.

## Why the Royal British Legion

Both Mum and our Dad served in the Royal Navy before they had children, and that connection to the Armed Forces remained an important part of our family throughout our lives. Gary and I later went to Naval boarding school, so the Navy and everything surrounding it has always been part of our family's story.

Mum was particularly passionate about Remembrance.

There is one memory from her final days that I don't think any of us will ever forget.

On Remembrance Sunday, 10th November 2024, Mum was extremely ill. Despite how poorly she was, she sat up in her bed, wearing her poppy, watching the Remembrance Sunday service at the Cenotaph.

Five days later, Mum passed away.

That memory has stayed with us. Even during those final days, Remembrance and what it represented still mattered enormously to her.

And when we decided that we wanted to do something significant in her memory, there really could only be one charity we wanted to support — the Royal British Legion.

## From where Mum passed away to where she was laid to rest

On 14th November 2026, my brother Gary, my eldest son Nathan, our family friend Stephen and I will get on our bikes outside Gary’s home in Castelnau, Barnes, London.

The same place where Mum spent her final days surrounded by her family and where she passed away.

From there, the four of us will attempt to cycle all the way from London to Yorkshire over two days.

And this isn't just a ride from London to Leeds.

Our journey has a very particular start and finish.

We will leave Castelnau on 14th November and, on 15th November 2026 — exactly two years to the day since Mum passed away — we will finish our ride at Yeadon Cemetery, where Mum was laid to rest.

And there is another reason why Yeadon is such a meaningful place for our journey to end.

Mum is buried there alongside her Mum and Dad, three of her brothers and two of her sisters — members of that large, close family who had been such an enormous part of her life.

There couldn't be a more meaningful finishing line for us.

It will be a long and difficult couple of days on the bike, in the middle of November, and there will undoubtedly be times when we're cold, tired, hurting and wondering why on earth we decided to do it.

But we know exactly why we're doing it.

When things get difficult, we'll think about Mum. We'll think about what she went through, the strength she showed, the family she loved so much and the memories she left us with.

And we'll keep pedalling.

From the home where Mum spent her final days in London, all the way back to the family and the place in Yorkshire that meant so much to her.

If, by doing that, we can also raise as much money as possible for the Royal British Legion, a charity that meant so much to Mum, then every difficult mile will be worth it.

If you are able to sponsor us, however much or however little, it would mean an enormous amount to all of us and to our family.

This ride is for Janet.

Our Mum. Their Nan. A much-loved Sister.

**For Mum.** ❤️
